## Primary case: Google Workspace Agent/MCP economics

Google's official [usage tiers](https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/quota-usage-tiers) and [billing/pricing documentation](https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/quota-pricing) establish a real change. The research hypothesis is that excess tool calls, unnecessary full-content fetching and failed retries might be optimized.

The strongest counterexample is **a small absolute bill**. At an illustrative **$0.50 per 1,000 billable units** and **50% avoidable unit reduction**, saving **$99/month** requires **396,000 billable units/month**. At $0.10 per 1,000, the threshold is **1,980,000 units/month**. These are **conditional arithmetic examples**, not measured quotas or a confirmed customer's usage. Operations can have different unit weights; the Standard/Scaled tier and actual request mix matter.

**One proposed next test — not authorized:** Build a read-only, offline unit-economics comparison from the official pricing and public operation weights for three plausible workload profiles. For each, report absolute USD/month, Standard vs Scaled implications, possible unit reduction and native alternatives. Suggested time ≤1 hour, subject to human approval; no implementation of an MCP proxy, customer outreach or spending.

**Decision after:** Keep attention only if a reachable buyer segment can plausibly exceed the savings break-even and a meaningful cost gap remains after native optimizations. Otherwise WATCH/DROP. Never interpret source `HYPOTHESIS_READY` as paid validation.
